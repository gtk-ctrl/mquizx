Three Doors and a Detective

Difficulty ★★☆about 5 minLogic PuzzleProbabilitySwitching
Treasure behind one of three doors. You pick one and the host opens an empty door. Should you switch?

Case Record

Behind one of three doors (1, 2, 3) is treasure, and the other two are empty. The host knows where the treasure is.

The contestant picks door 1. The host always opens an empty door among the other two, and always offers a switch. This time he opens door 3, which is empty, and asks, "Would you like to switch to door 2?"

If you switch to door 2, what is the probability that you find the treasure?

The Truth

Share my result

2/3

The answer is: 2/3.

The chance that your original pick, door 1, has the treasure is 1/3. So the chance that the treasure is behind door 2 or door 3 is 2/3. Because the host knows where the treasure is and always opens an empty door, all of that 2/3 moves onto the one unopened door, door 2. Switching wins with probability 2/3.

It is tempting to say "two doors left, so 1/2." But the host knows where the treasure is and opens an empty door on purpose, so the probabilities are not equal.

Key Points

  • Your first pick still has probability 1/3
  • The host deliberately opens an empty door
  • Switching collects the remaining 2/3

Why people get fooled

Intuition says "two doors left, so fifty-fifty."
